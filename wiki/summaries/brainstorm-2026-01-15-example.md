# Idea Register — 2026-01-15

## Session Overview

2026-01-15 · 3 ideas (1 active / 1 parked / 1 discarded) · Top idea: [[ideas/community-tool-library]]. A session about turning idle objects and skills on a single street into shared, searchable inventory — circling the same cold-start and trust problem from two angles.

## Ideas

### Community Tool Library → [[ideas/community-tool-library]]
**Status:** active  
**Lenses:** audience, market-gap, compounding  
**Viability:** 2/3/2

A neighborhood platform for lending and borrowing rarely-used tools. Targets cost-conscious homeowners and renters who don't want to buy a tool they'll use twice. Core value: turn idle objects on your street into a shared, searchable inventory.

**Key insight:** The software is easy; cold-starting enough local inventory that a search returns something useful is the whole game. Differentiation lives in solving the first-N-listings problem.

---

### Skill Swap Board → [[ideas/skill-swap-board]]
**Status:** parked  
**Lenses:** audience, differentiation  
**Viability:** 2/2/1

Same neighborhood, same trust-and-locality problem, but the unit of exchange is skills (fix a bike for an hour of drywall patching) instead of objects. Parked as a possible second surface once the tool side proves the reciprocity model.

**Key insight:** Shares the tool library's trust infrastructure, so it's cheap to add later — but on its own it has the same cold-start problem with none of the head start.

---

### Paid peer-to-peer tool rental marketplace
**Status:** discarded  
**Lenses:** monetization, risk

A fee-per-rental marketplace taking a cut of each transaction.

**Why discarded:** Pricing kills the neighborly, reciprocal feel that makes people willing to list at all, and insurance/liability overhead is too heavy for a v1.

## Patterns

### Recurring weaknesses
Distinctiveness scored weakest across the board — none of the three ideas is hard to copy at the software layer. The defensibility, where it exists, comes from accumulated local inventory and trust, not from the product itself.

### Recurring strengths
Strong instinct to find the smallest unit that has to work first (one street, not a city) before generalizing — and a willingness to discard the monetization variant when it conflicted with the behavior the system depends on.

### Thematic through-line
Coordinating underused local resources — objects, then skills — among neighbors who don't yet trust each other.

### Most promising idea
**[[ideas/community-tool-library]]** — Clearest motivated audience and a visible path to v1, with a real (if hard) compounding asset in local inventory. The honest read: it lives or dies on the cold-start, and that question is still open.

### Most interesting discard *(omit if none)*
**Paid marketplace** — Ruled out for v1, but a fee model may be worth revisiting once trust and density exist, as a sustainability layer rather than the core loop.

### Unasked question
How do you get the first ~30 tools listed on one block before there's an app worth pointing people to?
