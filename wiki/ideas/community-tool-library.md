# Community Tool Library

## Concept

A neighborhood platform for lending and borrowing rarely-used tools — drills, ladders, tile saws, pressure washers. The target user is a cost-conscious homeowner or renter who doesn't want to buy a tool they'll use twice. The core value proposition: turn the idle objects already sitting in garages on your street into a shared, searchable inventory.

## History

- **2026-01-15** ([[summaries/brainstorm-2026-01-15-example]]) — status: active — Introduced from "why does every house own a pressure washer they use twice a year?" Identified cold-start of local inventory as the central risk.

## Viability Assessment

**Desirability / Feasibility / Distinctiveness: 2/3/2**

- **Desirability (2):** Clear, relatable pain, but unproven that people will list their own tools for strangers nearby.
- **Feasibility (3):** A searchable local listing app is well-trodden; path to v1 is visible.
- **Distinctiveness (2):** Easy to copy at the software layer; the only durable edge is accumulated local inventory and trust.

## Strengths

A genuinely compounding asset (each listed tool makes the next search more likely to succeed) and an audience that can be reached one street at a time rather than all at once.

## Open Questions

1. How do you get the first ~30 tools listed on one block before there's an app worth pointing people to?
2. What enforces trust and return of borrowed items without heavy formal infrastructure?
3. Does density on one street actually translate to a repeatable playbook for the next street?
