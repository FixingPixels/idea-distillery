## Context

Thinking about the stuff that sits idle in a garage — a tile saw used once, a ladder
borrowed more than it's owned. Started from "why do five houses on one street each own
a pressure washer they use twice a year?" and explored a couple of ways to fix that.

## Ideas floated

- **Community Tool Library** — a neighborhood app for lending and borrowing rarely-used
  tools. Search "drill," see who two streets over has one free this weekend. The recurring
  worry: the app is the easy part; getting enough tools listed locally that a search returns
  something is the whole game. Talked about seeding inventory from a single enthusiastic
  block before opening it wider.

- **Skill Swap Board** — same neighborhood, but for skills instead of objects: someone who
  can fix a bike trades an hour for someone who can patch drywall. Felt adjacent to the tool
  library — same trust and locality problem, different unit of exchange. Parked it as a
  possible second surface once the tool side proves the trust model.

- **Paid peer-to-peer tool rental marketplace** (discarded) — charge a fee per rental, take
  a cut. Ruled out for now: pricing kills the neighborly, reciprocal feel that makes people
  willing to list at all, and the insurance/liability overhead is heavy for a v1.

## Constraints / framing mentioned

- Has to work at the scale of one street first; a sparse city-wide map is worthless.
- Reciprocity and trust matter more than payments at this stage.
- Wants something a single motivated neighbor could bootstrap, not a VC-scale launch.

## Open thread for next time

How do you get the first ~30 tools listed on one block without an app to point to yet?
